⚠️ The Gender Split for 1 and 2!

When counting objects, numbers **1** and **2** change depending on if the noun is **boy (masculine)** or **girl (feminine/neuter)**!

Boy Nouns (Masculine)

We use **un** (1) and **doi** (2):

  • un an (1 year)
  • doi câini (2 dogs)
  • doi băieți (2 boys)
Girl & Plural Copycat Nouns

We use **o** (1) and **două** (2):

  • o fată (1 girl)
  • două pisici (2 cats)
  • două mere (2 apples - Plural Neuter)

🍕 2. The Teen Adventure (11-19)

Forming teen numbers is like a puzzle! We use a math equation: **Unit + spre + zece** (which literally means *"Unit towards ten"*!).

Let's look at the formula in action:

treisprezece
➔
thirteen (3 + towards + 10)
optsprezece
➔
eighteen (8 + towards + 10)

⚠️ The Secret Teen Mutations:

A few numbers change slightly when they join the teens group. Watch out for these sneaky words:

  • 11: unsprezece (uses *un* instead of *unu*)
  • 12 (Boys): doisprezece / 12 (Girls/Neuter): douăsprezece
  • 14: paisprezece (changes *patru* to *pai*)
  • 16: șaisprezece (changes *șase* to *șai*)

🎈 3. Climbing the Tens (20-99)

Tens are super easy! We just use the plural form of ten, which is zeci. The formula is: **Unit + zeci**!

treizeci
➔
thirty (3 tens)
cincizeci
➔
fifty (5 tens)

Note: For 20, we always use the feminine/neuter *două*: douăzeci (twenty).

How about compound numbers? (like 25, 42, 87)

In Romanian, we connect the tens and units using the word și (and). The formula is: **Ten + și + Unit**!

douăzeci și cinci
➔
twenty-five (twenty and five)
patruzeci și doi / două
➔
forty-two (forty and two)

🌳 4. The Hundreds Club (100-999)

One hundred is a feminine noun in Romanian: o sută (a hundred). When we have more than one hundred, we use the plural form sute!

o sută
➔
one hundred
două sute
➔
two hundred
cinci sute
➔
five hundred

To write big numbers, we just string them together: Hundreds + Tens + și + Unit!

o sută douăzeci și cinci
➔
125 (one hundred twenty and five)

⭐ 5. Thousands & the Magic "DE" Rule!

One thousand is o mie. Multiple thousands are called mii. They work just like hundreds:

o mie
➔
one thousand
două mii
➔
two thousand
👑 The Magic "DE" Rule (Super Important!)

When we count items using numbers in Romanian, a special rule changes how we connect the number to the noun:

  • If the number ends in 1 to 19: We connect the number **directly** to the noun. No helper needed!
  • If the number ends in **20 or more**: We **must** put the magic helper word de between the number and the noun!
No "de" (ends in 1-19)
  • 5 mere
    ➔ cinci mere
  • 15 mii de oameni
    ➔ cincisprezece mii de oameni
  • 1,012 cărți
    ➔ o mie doisprezece cărți
With "de" (ends in 20+)
  • 20 de mere
    ➔ douăzeci de mere
  • 100 de cărți
    ➔ o sută de cărți
  • 2,000 de oameni
    ➔ două mii de oameni (2,000 ends in "mii", which is a noun, so we need "de")

🌌 6. Reaching the Millions! (1,000,000+)

Wow, we have traveled so far! Now we have reached the giant stars: the Millions!

  • 1,000,000: un milion (neuter singular)
  • 2,000,000: două milioane (plural)
  • 5,000,000: cinci milioane (plural)

Millions follow the exact same **"de" rule** when modifying nouns, because "milion" is a noun too!

două milioane de stele
➔
two million stars (2 < 20, so no "de" inside the number, but we need "de" before "stele")
douăzeci de milioane de stele
➔
twenty million stars (20 >= 20, so we need "de" after "douăzeci", and "de" after "milioane")

🐱 7. The Copycat Nouns (Neuters)

In Romanian, there is a special class of nouns called **Neuter Nouns**. We call them the **Copycat Nouns**!

  • When there is **only one** (Singular), they copy **boys**!
  • When there are **two or more** (Plural), they copy **girls**!

Let's look at the apple (un măr / două mere):

un măr
➔
one apple (copies masculine "un")
două mere
➔
two apples (copies feminine "două")
